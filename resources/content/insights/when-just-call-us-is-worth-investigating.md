A phone conversation can be exactly the right experience. A customer may need advice, the request may have important exceptions, or the consequences of a mistake may justify a careful human check. The goal is not to remove people from every interaction.

The useful question is narrower: **does this particular task need a conversation every time?**

## When calling makes sense

Calls are often appropriate when the work depends on judgment, trust, urgency, sensitive information, or a choice that is difficult to explain in a form. They can also be the best accommodation for a customer who prefers to speak with someone.

A process should not be changed merely because it looks old-fashioned. If the conversation is valuable and the burden is reasonable, **Leave Alone** may be the right decision.

## When the instruction may be a signal

“Just call us” may be worth investigating when the underlying request is routine: changing a mailing address, checking a straightforward status, rescheduling within clear rules, or requesting information already held in a system.

That public instruction is an observation—not proof of a business problem. It does not reveal how many people call, how long the work takes, whether customers object, or whether the current software offers another option.

## Questions before proposing a change

- What is the customer trying to accomplish?
- What does the staff member do after answering?
- Which rules and exceptions require judgment?
- How often does the request occur?
- What goes wrong, if anything?
- Can the current system already handle it?
- Would another channel improve access, or merely add a channel to maintain?

Evidence might show that clearer website copy is enough. It might support a configuration change, a simple request form, or a native self-service feature. It could also show that a call remains the clearest and most economical option.

The phrase is a prompt to investigate, not a diagnosis. Start with what can be observed, identify what remains unknown, and let evidence determine whether anything should change.
